### Chapter 1 — Command and Staff Organization

#### The Commanding General (p. 1-3)

> "The commander is responsible for everything the command does or fails to do.
> The commander is the final authority on all matters in the command and is
> personally responsible for the health, welfare, morale, and discipline of
> their command."

#### Chief of Staff Authority (pp. 1-3, 1-18)

> "The chief of staff is not a member of the commander's personal staff but a
> member of the general staff. The chief of staff is the commander's principal
> assistant with **delegated management authority** over other general staff
> members and **cognizant authority** over special staff members."
>
> "The COS will establish and manage the battle rhythm of the organization and
> orchestrate briefings and meetings for the commander."

#### Staff Secretary (p. 1-18)

> "As an administrative assistant to the COS, the staff secretary's
> responsibilities include: Maintain the temporary office of record for all
> incoming correspondence of the commander and the COS... Ensure all interested
> staff sections receive and act on information routed to the COS... Plan and
> supervise conferences chaired by the commander."

#### Information Management Officer — IMO (p. 1-8)

> "The IMO under the auspices of the command group develops a clearly defined
> and well executed **information management plan (IMP)** to ensure quality
> information gets to the right place on time and in a form quickly usable by
> its intended recipients."
>
> "Facilitate battle rhythm coordination, routine reporting, and commander's
> critical information requirement development and tracking using IM systems."

#### Red Team (p. 1-13)

> "Red team individuals support the inclusion of independent, critical thought,
> and alternative perspectives... They help reduce risk and expand opportunities
> by exposing plans and concepts to rigorous examination... Each MEF requires a
> red team that reports directly to the COS as a special staff officer."

#### G-Staff Section Summary

| Section | Title          | Key Responsibilities                                          |
| ------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **G-1** | Personnel      | Manning, awards, personnel accounting, casualty reporting     |
| **G-2** | Intelligence   | All-source intel, CCIR/PIR, ISR coordination                  |
| **G-3** | Operations     | MCPP, OPORD, COC, future ops, training management             |
| **G-4** | Logistics      | CSS planning, supply, maintenance, transport, health services |
| **G-5** | Plans          | Long-range/contingency planning, CONPLANs, OPLANs             |
| **G-6** | Communications | CIS, networks, spectrum management, cybersecurity             |
| **G-7** | COMMSTRAT      | Information warfare, public narrative, inform operations      |
| **G-8** | Financial      | Budget, financial management, cost accounting                 |

#### AC/S G-2 — Intelligence (p. 1-22)

> "The AC/S G-2 is the principal staff assistant for all intelligence matters
> and is responsible for: Providing all-source intelligence support...
> Developing and maintaining intelligence databases and products... Identifying
> intelligence gaps and providing all-source analysis."
>
> "PIR [Priority Intelligence Requirements] — What does the commander need to
> know about the enemy and the environment to make decisions?"
>
> "CCIR [Commander's Critical Information Requirements] — the threshold of
> information needed for the commander to change plans or operations
> significantly."

#### AC/S G-3 — Operations (p. 1-28)

> "The AC/S G-3 is the principal staff assistant for all operational matters.
> Responsibilities include: Conduct and/or contribute to the MCPP... Develop
> COAs... Prepare and staff OPORDs... Operate the COC [Combat Operations > > > >
>
> > Center]..."
>
> "Future Operations Officer. The future operations section is the focal point
> of the Marine Corps Planning Process (MCPP) activities... fully integrates the
> other staff section plans officers, warfighting function representatives, and
> subordinate unit representatives into the planning process."

#### AC/S G-4 — Logistics (p. 1-37)

> "The AC/S G-4 is the principal staff assistant for all logistic matters...
> plans, coordinates and supervises the provision of CSS in the areas of supply,
> maintenance, transportation, health services, engineer support, landing
> support, materials handling, food services, mortuary affairs, and host-nation
> support."
>
> "Identify CSS requirements and prioritize CSS through close and continuous
> coordination with the AC/S G-3."

#### AC/S G-5 — Plans (p. 1-21)

> "The AC/S G-5 is the principal staff assistant for long-range and contingency
> planning... The AC/S G-5 conducts long-range planning and interfaces with
> higher headquarters for theater plans and operations."
>
> "The planning section integrates all staff sections into the planning process
> through Operation Planning Teams (OPTs)."

#### AC/S G-6 — Communications (p. 1-45)

> "Monitor the status of CIS... Manage spectrum assignments to include radio
> frequencies, satellite communications, and call words... Supervise the
> planning and execution of CO, defensive cyberspace operations (DCO), and
> information assurance... Prepare Annex K (Combat Information Systems) to the
> OPLAN/OPORD."

#### AC/S G-7 — COMMSTRAT (p. 1-49)

> "The AC/S G-7 works in concert with the MEF information group to coordinate,
> integrate, and employ Information Warfare capabilities... Advise the commander
> and staff on the probable public impact of command decisions/policy... Provide
> accurate, timely, and credible communication – in coordination with other
> information and combat capabilities – to attack the enemy's will to fight."

#### AC/S G-8 — Financial (p. 1-50)

> "The AC/S G-8 maintains financial resources required to equip, train, deploy,
> and maintain combat ready MAGTFs/forces... maintains sound financial systems,
> processes, and internal controls to ensure efficient, effective, and legal
> expenditure of financial resources."

### Chapter 2 — Staff Action

#### Completed Staff Action (p. 2-21)

> "Completed staff action on a problem results in the presentation of the
> problem, conclusions, and any recommended COAs **in such form that all the
> commander needs to do is indicate approval or disapproval.** It includes the
> coordination of the action with all affected staff sections before the
> commander's decision."
>
> "All matters brought to the commander for decision should be presented as
> completed staff action."

#### Action Officers (p. 2-2)

> "The term action officer refers to staff members that shape information and
> submit recommendations to senior decision makers for approval. The AO
> synthesizes the facts, summarizes the issues, presents feasible alternatives,
> and recommends one of them. AOs anticipate non-concurrences and try to resolve
> as many as possible before staffing the final product."

#### Parallel Planning (p. 2-19)

> "The need to generate and maintain operational tempo sometimes drives
> organizations to use parallel planning rather than sequential planning when
> conducting operations. Units at various levels of command plan concurrently
> based on the mission and the next higher commander's intent... We mitigate
> this risk through collaborative planning. Planners at all levels can plan
> concurrently and in concert with one another."

#### Military Briefing Types (pp. 2-15/16)

> "Briefings usually take the form of information, decision, staff, or mission
> briefings."

| Type                     | Purpose                                        | Desired Outcome                              |
| ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| **Information Briefing** | Present facts / current situation              | Comprehension — no decision required         |
| **Decision Briefing**    | Present COAs for commander's choice            | A decision / answer to a question            |
| **Staff Briefing**       | Rapid group dissemination (e.g., daily update) | Coordinated effort across sections           |
| **Mission Briefing**     | Last-minute info before execution              | Full understanding of operational conditions |

#### Liaison Officers — LNO (p. 2-18)

> "Organizations exchange LNOs or liaison teams to establish close, continuous,
> physical communication among forces... Liaison personnel typically perform
> four basic functions: **Monitor, Coordinate, Advise, Assist.**"
>
> "Liaison personnel are not substitutes for delivering critical information
> through normal C2 channels... Liaisons do not have the authority to make
> decisions for their commander without coordination and approval."

#### Reachback (p. 2-20)

> "Electronic capabilities can also provide deployed forces access to virtual
> staffs through the use of a global network of military, government civilians,
> and consultant specialists... In many cases, it is not possible to deploy
> personnel with the complete range of expertise needed to address all of the
> problems that will be encountered in forward areas."

### Chapter 3 — Staff Action Papers and Formats

#### Position/Decision Paper (p. 3-1)

> "An AO prepares a position/decision paper to develop, recommend, and obtain an
> official position on a particular proposition. It includes a clear statement
> of why an official position/decision is required, essential background on the
> problem or subject, and a rationale for the recommended position."

#### Point Paper (p. 3-2)

> "A point paper is a concise document designed to present key points, facts,
> positions, or questions in a brief and orderly fashion... normally does not
> exceed one page."

#### Staff Study — Six-Part Format (pp. 3-3/4)

> "A staff study is a flexible, problem-solving procedure... usually organized
> using the following six main paragraph headings: 2. **Assumptions** —
> suppositions treated as true in absence of proof 3. **Facts Bearing on the
> Problem** — authenticated, relevant facts 4. **Discussion** — analyze the
> problem and consider options 5. **Conclusions** — select the best solution;
> must flow logically from discussion 6. **Action Recommended** — how
> conclusions can be implemented; commander approves"

### Chapter 4 — Command and Support Relationships

#### OPCON vs. TACON (p. 4-1)

> "**Operational Control (OPCON):** authority to perform those functions of
> command over subordinate forces involving organizing and employing commands
> and forces, assigning tasks, designating objectives, and giving authoritative
> direction necessary to accomplish the mission."
>
> "**Tactical Control (TACON):** authority over forces limited to the detailed
> direction and control of movements or maneuvers within the operational area
> necessary to accomplish assigned missions or tasks."

#### Support Relationships — Four Categories (p. 4-1)

| Category                              | Definition                                                              |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Direct Support (DS)**               | Support given to a specific subordinate force; report to that commander |
| **General Support (GS)**              | Support given to the whole force, not a specific subdivision            |
| **Reinforcing**                       | Augments another unit of the same type                                  |
| **General Support-Reinforcing (GSR)** | GS to the whole force while reinforcing a specific unit                 |

## Doctrinal Echelon Designators

| Echelon                  | Prefix | Example       |
| ------------------------ | ------ | ------------- |
| Major Command (MEF, MEB) | **G-** | G-2, G-3, G-4 |
| Battalion / Squadron     | **S-** | S-2, S-3, S-4 |
| Joint / Combined Force   | **J-** | J-2, J-3, J-4 |

> **Implication for Suitcase AI:** Strategic-level AI agents use G-designators.
> Swarm-level (Gas Town) agents use S-designators to preserve doctrinal echelon
> distinction.

## Relationship to Suitcase AI Plans

See [plans/business_case_s_staff.md](../plans/business_case_s_staff.md) for the
full AI-to-doctrine role mapping. A detailed architecture mapping derived from
this document is in
[plans/s_staff_deliverables_and_harness_architecture.md](../plans/s_staff_deliverables_and_harness_architecture.md).

**Key architectural principles drawn from MCTP 3-30A:**

1. **CoS has delegated authority** over G-Staff — not co-equal with the AG
2. **Staff Secretary** is a dedicated task-routing role, distinct from AG
3. **Red Team** is a Special Staff officer reporting direct to COS
4. **G-7 (COMMSTRAT)** and **G-8 (Financial)** are full staff sections, not
   sub-functions of G-3/G-4
5. **Completed Staff Action** is the quality gate before the Commander sees a
   decision
6. **Staff estimates run in parallel** (G-2, G-4, G-5 concurrently), not
   sequentially
7. **Battle Rhythm** is an explicit COS responsibility driving recurring
   synchronization events
8. **LNOs** are the doctrinal pattern for external system integration adapters
9. **S-designators** apply at the swarm/battalion echelon, not G-level
